# MakeEase go-to-market positioning

## One-line position

> **MakeEase is the approval-first support workflow for small B2B SaaS teams that need faster drafts without giving an opaque bot the final say.**

## Message hierarchy

### Lead with the outcome

**Turn repetitive support threads into drafts your team can review.**

MakeEase uses approved policies and product context to prepare a triage result, response draft, and escalation recommendation. A reviewer sees the evidence and decides what happens next.

### Prove trust with four concrete promises

1. **Grounded:** use customer-approved policies and context, with visible provenance and uncertainty.
2. **Reviewable:** approval-before-send is the default product boundary.
3. **Observable:** keep the request, context, output, decision, usage, and failure state together.
4. **Bounded:** enforce scope, attempts, budgets, and a hard capacity stop.

### State the live boundary

At launch, MakeEase does not automatically send customer messages, issue refunds, deploy code, change access, or move money. An approved result may be saved as a Gmail draft only when the production Gmail capability is explicitly scoped and verified; the human reviewer remains responsible for sending.

## Who to target

### Buyer and champion

- **Economic buyer:** founder, COO, or Head of Operations.
- **Champion:** support lead or the person currently reviewing replies.
- **End user:** the operator who triages threads and approves/edit/rejects drafts.

### Trigger events

- founder-led support has become a recurring interruption;
- a new support hire needs product/policy context quickly;
- repeat categories consume senior attention;
- response-time pressure is rising but the team rejects unsupervised sending; or
- a support lead wants a consistent audit/review path across a small inbox.

## What not to say

Do not lead with “Business Automation OS,” “AI employees,” “autonomous support,” “replace your helpdesk,” or “cheapest AI support.” Do not claim to beat all researched competitors. Mature vendors have deeper ticketing, omnichannel, connector, analytics, RPA, and enterprise infrastructure.

The credible comparison is narrower:

> MakeEase gives a small team one understandable support-review loop with explicit evidence, approval, and cost boundaries. It does not try to replace a mature helpdesk or win on connector count.

## Competitive framing from the research

| Alternative | What it does well | MakeEase response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Zendesk / Intercom / Gorgias / adjacent helpdesks | Mature support workspace, channels, routing, knowledge, and agent-assist or resolution products | Start beside the existing inbox/helpdesk with one reviewable support workflow; win on a smaller, legible control loop, not helpdesk replacement. |
| Zapier / Make | Broad app catalogs and approachable workflow composition | Make the first job narrower; show scope, evidence, risk, approval, and cost before work is used. |
| n8n / UiPath | Technical control, self-hosting, extensibility, or RPA | Keep the default path nontechnical and bounded; defer arbitrary code/browser automation and own the review experience. |
| Lindy / general AI coworkers | Fast natural-language automation and broad assistant framing | Replace vague autonomy with a typed support outcome, approved context, durable review, and explicit no-side-effect behavior. |
| Agentforce / enterprise suites | CRM-native actions, governance, and enterprise distribution | Stay vendor-neutral and small-team focused; do not claim equal enterprise controls before they exist. |

These are positioning implications from the dated competitor briefs in `research/competitors/`, not universal performance or price claims.

## Offer and sales motion

### First offer: paid guided pilot

For the first 5–10 design partners, offer a 30-day guided pilot at the approved hypothesis of **$299 one time**:

- one Gmail inbox or label;
- one support workflow;
- two named reviewers;
- 150 support review packets with a hard stop;
- policy/context setup and four short weekly reviews within the bounded service allowance;
- approval-before-send throughout; and
- explicit opt-in to the $149 monthly Support package, with no automatic renewal.

Show an unconnected, synthetic demo before the pilot. Do not offer unlimited live AI, automatic send, or a permanent free production tier.

### Public launch package (after gates)

MakeEase Support is the launch pricing hypothesis: **$149/workspace/month**, one inbox/label, three reviewers, one workflow, 500 support threads prepared for review per month, 90 days of searchable history/export, business-hours email support, no automatic overage, and no automatic customer-facing send. See `PRICING-FINAL-DECISION.md` for capacity, expiry, and billing controls.

### Channels

- founder/operator networks and B2B SaaS communities;
- referrals from fractional COOs, support consultants, and implementation partners;
- targeted outbound triggered by support-hiring, volume, or founder-support pain; and
- a support-inbox audit that uses consented, redacted sample threads.

Use founder-led, consultative selling for the first 10–20 accounts. The sale is a workflow diagnosis and trust conversation, not a template-catalog tour.

## Qualification

### Qualify in

- recurring support volume and repeatable categories;
- one accessible decision maker and one policy owner;
- a first inbox/label that can be scoped narrowly;
- willingness to review early outputs and keep approval-before-send;
- a baseline they can measure: review time, response time, edit/reject/escalation, or failure patterns; and
- a buyer who values accountability and predictable capacity over full autonomy.

### Qualify out or defer

- “send everything automatically” as a day-one requirement;
- no reviewer or policy owner;
- a request for omnichannel/helpdesk replacement;
- finance, refunds, engineering deployment, browser automation, or broad integration as the first job; or
- a volume/cost profile that cannot fit a hard cap and measured support envelope.

## Proof plan

The pilot must measure, not infer:

- first approved draft and three distinct threads reviewed in seven days;
- prepared packets, completed reviews, approval-as-is, edit, reject, escalation, and failure rates;
- review latency and customer-provided response-time baseline;
- provider/model cost per prepared packet, including tests, retries, failures, and support;
- cap hits, pause events, and duplicate/reconciliation exceptions; and
- pilot-to-paid conversion, weekly repeat usage, and week-8 retention.

Do not publish “hours saved,” “resolution,” “deflection,” recovered revenue, or percentage ROI unless the customer supplies a documented baseline and the metric clearly distinguishes prepared drafts from executed outcomes.

## Landing-page copy starter

### Headline

**Faster support drafts. A human still decides.**

### Subhead

MakeEase prepares policy-grounded replies and escalation recommendations from your support context, then puts every result in a review queue with visible evidence and bounded usage.

### CTA

**Book a support inbox audit**

### Reassurance

Start with a synthetic, unconnected demo. In the sandbox, nothing is emailed, posted, charged, or changed outside the workspace.

## Expansion gate

Keep the support position until at least ten paying workspaces use it weekly, week-8 retention and repeat volume are measured, quality/cost/failure rates are understood, and at least three customers independently request the same adjacent workflow. Expand first into a nearby customer-operations job that shares inbox and policy context; do not broaden to an OS category based on demo breadth alone.
